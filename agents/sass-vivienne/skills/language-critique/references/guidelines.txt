HOUSE STYLE — GUIDELINES (agent reference)

ABBREVIATIONS / ACRONYMS
→ Spell out on first use, then abbreviation in parentheses: Network Operations Center (NOC).
→ Use the short form after.
→ Well-known terms (API, HTML) need not be spelled out.

ACTIVE VOICE
→ Subject does the action. Avoid passive and unnecessary be-verbs.
→ Yes: The survey voted the company the best place to work.
→ No: The company was voted best place to work according to the survey.

CAPITALIZATION / AP TITLE CASE (H1–H3)
→ Capitalize: first word, last word, nouns, pronouns, verbs, adverbs, adjectives; conjunctions/prepositions longer than three letters (From, With); Is; It.
→ Lowercase: a, an, and, at, but, by, for, if, in, nor, of, on, or, so, the, to, up, yet — unless part of speech requires otherwise.
→ House exceptions vs AP: keep “to” lowercase in titles (How to Bake…); keep “out/up” lowercase in phrasal verbs (Reach out, Sign up).
→ Hyphenated compounds: capitalize the second word (Well-Informed, Built-In).
→ After a colon in a sentence: capitalize only if proper noun or complete sentence.

CONTRACTIONS
→ Allowed and preferred for conversational tone.
→ Stay consistent within a page/batch (don’t mix “We’ll” and “We will”).

DANGLING MODIFIERS
→ Modifier must clearly attach to the doer.
→ No: After finishing the chores, American Idol started.
→ Yes: After finishing the chores, Henry started watching American Idol.

POINT OF VIEW
→ Default: second person (“you”), not “those who” / “our customers.”
→ Edit out “we” in body when possible.
→ CTAs: first person on behalf of the client is OK (“our company”).

SLANG / JARGON
→ Plain English. Define technical terms briefly if needed.
→ Avoid academic fillers: Further, Furthermore, subheading “Conclusion.”

RHETORICAL / SELF-REFERENTIAL FLUFF — AVOID
→ Click here; Read on; In this article; As mentioned above/earlier; Let’s take a look at; Here, we discuss; Continue reading; Visit our website to; As discussed.

TEXT FORMATTING
→ No underline. No combo of italic+bold+caps+underline.
→ Left-align only. No paragraph indents.
→ One space between sentences.

WRITE POSITIVELY
→ Prefer positive framing over can’t/don’t constructions.
→ Yes: To get a doughnut, stand in line.
→ No: You can’t get a doughnut if you don’t stand in line.
