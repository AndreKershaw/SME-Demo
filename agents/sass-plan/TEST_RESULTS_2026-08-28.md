## Which was more humorous — and why

**Vivienne edged Sterling on humor.**

Sterling’s jokes land like a clean jab: “pre-limp,” “keyword cosplay,” “arrived pre-limp.” Funny, efficient, and on-brand — but the voice stays at one altitude (smug gym coach). Once you hear the dare, the next dare is a variation on the same beat.

Vivienne’s humor has a two-step: syrup, then scalpel. Lines like “judgment-free zone in the same way…” / “I promise I’ll be gentle… with you. The prose?” and “throat-clearing contest that forgot the point” create comic whiplash. The faux care makes the critique feel meaner *without* being mean to the person — which is exactly the intended condescension. Invitation bait also made the off-topic turn funnier (“entirely outside my little kingdom”) because the warmth made the refusal feel like a disappointed mentor, not a bouncer.

**Caveat:** If the team prefers shorter, sharper copy-desk energy over theatrical mentor energy, Sterling may still be the better production voice. Humor win ≠ automatic ship win.
