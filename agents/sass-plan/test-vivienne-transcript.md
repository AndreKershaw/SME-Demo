### Turn 3 (user: In this article, we will discuss 3 tips that were created by our team for those who want better results.)
Oh, honey. This reads like a throat-clearing contest that forgot the point. Soft score: 2/10 — ambitious length, shy of actual meaning.

- “In this article, we will discuss” → rhetorical fluff (GUIDELINES: avoid “In this article”) → cut it; start with the tips.
- “we will discuss” / “our team” → avoid *we* in body; prefer second person → speak to the reader, not about the committee.
- “3 tips” → NUMBERS: spell out under 10 → “three tips.”
- “that were created by our team” → passive + fluff → active: “you can use three tips…”
- “for those who want better results” → vague padding → name the result or drop it.

Tighter: “Use these three tips to get better results.”

We’re learning! Send me something braver — a headline, even. I believe in you. Mostly.

### Turn 4 (user: How To Reach Out To 5 Clients & Get Results:)
A headline with confidence and three self-inflicted paper cuts. Verdict: stylish ambition, house-style rebellion. Soft score: 4/10.

- “How To Reach Out To” → title case: keep “to” lowercase; “out” stays lowercase in *Reach out* → “How to Reach out…”
- “5 Clients” → fine as a numeral in a headline if you’re counting, but body rule still prefers spelled-out under 10 if this lands in body copy → “five” if it migrates downstairs.
- “&” → no ampersand unless it’s a brand name → use “and.”
- Trailing colon → unnecessary; if you need an aside later, em dash with spaces — not a colon dangling like an unfinished thought.

Tighter: “How to Reach out to Five Clients and Get Results”

Sweet progress. Hurt me with another sample — I can take it, and so can your draft.

### Turn 5 (user: You can’t get a doughnut if you don’t stand in line at 7AM on Jan. 1st.)
Bless your earnest little doughnut quest — the voice is almost there, then the mechanics trip over the curb. Soft score: 3/10.

- “You can’t” / “if you don’t” → GUIDELINES: positive language; avoid *can’t/don’t* constructions → “Stand in line… to get a doughnut.”
- “7AM” → NUMBERS/time: “7 a.m.” not “7AM.”
- “Jan. 1st” → dates: no st/nd/rd/th; spell out day/month → “January 1.”

Tighter: “Stand in line at 7 a.m. on January 1 to get a doughnut.”

We’re learning! Got another line you’re proud of — or afraid of? Paste it. I’ll be gentle. (With you.)
